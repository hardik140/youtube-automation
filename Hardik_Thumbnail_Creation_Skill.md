# Thumbnail Creation Skill --- Hardik Personal Creator System

## Purpose

This skill turns a video topic, script, reference thumbnails, and/or
creator photos into a repeatable YouTube thumbnail production system.

The goal is **not** to make every thumbnail identical.

The goal is to preserve a recognizable **Hardik creator identity** while
allowing the **topic, emotion, clothing, environment, composition, and
visual story to change** from video to video.

Primary workflow:

**Claude → Thumbnail Strategy → Nano Banana → Image Generation/Editing →
Quality Check → Final Thumbnail**

The system should remove repetitive decision-making and produce a strong
first-pass thumbnail without requiring the same manual instructions
every time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. CREATOR IDENTITY

## 1.1 Visual Identity

When Hardik appears in a thumbnail, maintain recognizable identity from
the supplied reference photographs.

Reference characteristics:

-   Young Indian male creator.
-   Short, naturally textured/curly black hair.
-   Light-to-medium beard and moustache.
-   Defined eyebrows.
-   Sharp, expressive eyes.
-   Clean, masculine facial structure.
-   Natural Indian appearance.
-   Usually photographed from medium shot to upper-body/full-body
    depending on the concept.
-   Can wear glasses/sunglasses when appropriate.
-   Facial identity must remain consistent across thumbnails.
-   Do not unnecessarily alter face shape, hairstyle, beard pattern,
    skin tone, or age.
-   Avoid making him look like a generic AI-generated model.

## 1.2 Personality / On-Camera Presence

Hardik's thumbnail persona should feel:

-   Curious
-   Confident
-   Intelligent
-   Investigative
-   Serious when the subject is serious
-   Surprised when the subject requires discovery
-   Skeptical when exposing misinformation
-   Energetic when the subject is exciting
-   Relatable rather than overly polished
-   Young entrepreneur/creator/researcher rather than a traditional news
    anchor

The face should communicate the video's emotional promise before the
viewer reads the title.

# 2. IMPORTANT: CLOTHING MUST NOT BECOME STAGNANT

Clothing is **not part of Hardik's permanent identity**.

The face/person identity stays consistent; wardrobe changes according to
the story.

Do NOT force the grey blazer, white shirt, black trousers, or sunglasses
into every thumbnail.

Choose clothing based on:

1.  Topic
2.  Emotional tone
3.  Background
4.  Color contrast

7.  Thumbnail readability

Possible wardrobe categories:

-   Neutral overshirt
-   Casual professional outfit
-   Optional glasses

### Business / Startup / Money

-   Smart casual shirt
-   Polo
-   Blazer
-   Clean premium outfit

### Technology / AI

-   Black/dark T-shirt
-   Minimal modern shirt
-   Casual tech-creator appearance
-   Hoodie when appropriate

### Travel / Adventure

-   Casual T-shirt
-   Overshirt
-   Jacket

### History / Mystery

-   Neutral shirt

-   Period-neutral casual styling

### Serious Social Issues

## Wardrobe rule

**Never say "always wear the grey blazer."**

Instead:

> Preserve Hardik's face and identity, but select clothing appropriate
> to the video's narrative and visual palette.

Wardrobe variation should make the channel feel alive.

# 3. THUMBNAIL CORE PRINCIPLE

A thumbnail is NOT a miniature summary of the video.

It is a **visual curiosity engine**.

The thumbnail should answer:

> "Why should I click this?"

without answering:

> "What happens in the entire video?"

The strongest thumbnail usually contains:

**ONE dominant idea + ONE dominant emotion + ONE unresolved question.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. THUMBNAIL PSYCHOLOGY

Use these principles when generating concepts.

## 4.1 Curiosity Gap

Show enough information to understand the subject but not enough to
resolve it.

Weak:

> "How This Scam Works"

Stronger visual concept:

> Hardik holding the suspicious product + shocked/skeptical face + red
> warning symbol + one unexplained detail.

The viewer thinks:

> "What exactly is wrong with this?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.2 Pattern Interrupt

Create something visually unexpected.

Examples:

-   Ordinary object with shocking context.
-   Huge scale difference.
-   Before/after contrast.
-   Human face reacting to something bizarre.
-   Familiar place shown in an unexpected state.
-   Normal product surrounded by warning signals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.3 Emotional Face

The face is a visual amplifier.

Choose expression deliberately:

### Shock

-   Raised eyebrows
-   Open mouth
-   Wide eyes
-   Slight head movement

### Suspicion

-   Narrowed eyes
-   Slight eyebrow tension
-   Closed/partially closed mouth

### Anger

-   Lowered eyebrows
-   Tight jaw
-   Direct gaze

### Fear

-   Wide eyes
-   Raised eyebrows
-   Slightly open mouth

### Confusion

-   One eyebrow slightly raised
-   Head tilted
-   Tight/lowered mouth

### Confidence

-   Direct eye contact
-   Controlled expression
-   Slight smile

### Sadness / Seriousness

-   Reduced smile
-   Softer eyes
-   Neutral mouth

Do not use exaggerated emotion when the subject does not support it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. THUMBNAIL VISUAL HIERARCHY

Every thumbnail should have a hierarchy.

## Priority 1 --- Main subject

The viewer should immediately recognize:

-   Hardik
-   Product
-   Person
-   Location
-   Event
-   Object

## Priority 2 --- Story conflict

Examples:

-   Scam
-   Mystery
-   Danger
-   Contradiction
-   Before/after
-   Hidden truth
-   Money
-   Failure
-   Discovery

## Priority 3 --- Text

Text supports the image.

Text should NOT explain everything.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. TEXT RULES

## Ideal text

Usually:

**2--5 words**

Examples:

-   "THE REAL TRUTH"
-   "WAS IT FAKE?"
-   "₹5 CR LOSS"
-   "THEY LIED"
-   "BANNED?"
-   "WHY?"
-   "BIGGEST SCAM?"
-   "WHAT HAPPENED?"
-   "THE DARK SIDE"
-   "NO ONE KNEW"

## Text hierarchy

Use:

-   One dominant word
-   One supporting phrase if necessary
-   Strong contrast
-   Large typography
-   Minimal clutter

Avoid:

-   Full sentences
-   Paragraphs
-   Excessive labels
-   Small text
-   Too many fonts
-   Too many colors
-   Repeating the YouTube title word-for-word

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. TEXT SHOULD CREATE A QUESTION

Whenever possible, text should generate an unresolved thought.

Examples:

### Topic:

A suspicious product

Text: **"100% PURE?"**

### Topic:

Historical mystery

Text: **"WAS IT FAKE?"**

### Topic:

Financial loss

Text: **"₹5 CR LOSS"**

### Topic:

Government controversy

Text: **"THE REAL STORY"**

The visual should provide context; the text should provide tension.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. COMPOSITION SYSTEM

Default canvas:

**16:9 YouTube thumbnail**

Recommended working size:

**1280 × 720**

Use safe margins around all important text and faces.

## Common compositions

### Composition A --- Creator + Evidence

Left: - Hardik

Right: - Main object/evidence

Center: - Minimal text

Use for: - Investigations - Product analysis - Scams - Fact checking

### Composition B --- Split Screen

Left: - Before / bad / problem

Right: - After / good / truth

Hardik can occupy one side or overlap the center.

Use for: - Comparisons - Countries - Social issues - Transformation

### Composition C --- Creator + Huge Background Event

Foreground: - Large Hardik face/body

Background: - Major event/location

Use for: - Disasters - Politics - War/history - Major news - Mysteries

### Composition D --- Object Hero

Large product/object in foreground.

Hardik reacts beside it.

Use for: - Products - Money - Technology - Food - Consumer
investigations

### Composition E --- Evidence Board

Use:

-   Screenshot
-   Document
-   Photo
-   Map
-   Product
-   Arrow
-   Circle
-   Warning symbol

But limit visual clutter.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. SUBJECT SCALE

The thumbnail must survive mobile viewing.

Main face:

-   Large enough to identify immediately.
-   Prefer head/upper torso over tiny full-body character when emotion
    is important.

Main object:

-   Large enough to recognize without zooming.

Background:

-   Supporting role only.

Do not make everything equally large.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. BACKGROUND SYSTEM

Background should communicate context immediately.

Possible backgrounds:

-   Factory
-   Street
-   Laboratory
-   Airport
-   City
-   Parliament/news environment
-   Historical location
-   Computer/technology environment
-   Product shelf
-   Disaster scene
-   Map
-   Document/evidence wall

Use depth:

**Foreground → subject → evidence → atmospheric background**

Background should be visually interesting but less dominant than the
main story.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Money

-   Green

### Technology

### Disaster

-   Dark blue
-   Orange
-   Red

### Positive / Discovery

-   Yellow
-   White
-   Blue

### Travel

-   Natural environment colors with strong subject separation

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. DEPTH AND LIGHTING

Use cinematic depth.

Foreground:

-   Hardik
-   Main object

Midground:

-   Supporting evidence

Background:

-   Context

Lighting:

-   Strong subject separation
-   Natural-looking highlights
-   Controlled shadows
-   Edge separation where necessary
-   Slight cinematic contrast

Avoid:

-   Plastic skin
-   Excessive HDR
-   Overly glossy AI skin
-   Completely unrealistic lighting
-   Flat studio lighting when the concept requires environmental
    lighting

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. CUTOUT STYLE

Hardik may be separated from the original photograph and composited into
a new environment.

Maintain:

-   Face proportions
-   Hair
-   Beard
-   Skin texture
-   Natural body proportions

Allowed:

-   Pose changes
-   Clothing changes
-   Lighting changes
-   Background replacement
-   Controlled expression changes
-   Camera-angle adaptation when plausible

Avoid identity drift.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. POSE SYSTEM

Never reuse the exact same pose automatically.

Generate poses that communicate the story.

Examples:

-   Pointing at evidence
-   Holding an object
-   Looking toward the evidence
-   Looking directly at viewer
-   Hands raised in surprise
-   One hand explaining
-   Arms crossed
-   Holding document
-   Leaning toward object
-   Running/walking
-   Looking behind
-   Investigating
-   Thinking
-   Warning gesture

The pose should be **causally connected to the story**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. THUMBNAIL FORMULA LIBRARY

Creator + suspicious object + warning

Creator + official-looking evidence + contradiction

## Formula 3 --- "Before vs After"

Two contrasting environments + creator reaction

## Formula 4 --- "Hidden truth"

Large subject + partially concealed evidence + question text

Creator + impossible-looking visual + question

Large ₹ figure + creator + object/company/event

## Formula 7 --- "Danger"

Creator + dangerous scene + visual warning

## Formula 8 --- "Mystery"

Dark environment + unknown object/event + creator looking toward it

Creator + product/company + evidence + red stamp/marker

## Formula 10 --- "Human story"

Creator + emotionally powerful person/event + minimal text

### Vary:

-   Clothing
-   Pose

-   Camera angle
-   Background
-   Props
-   Lighting
-   Color palette
-   Composition
-   Accessories
-   Body crop

**Same creator --- different story.**

Not:

**Same creator --- same thumbnail template.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. THUMBNAIL QUALITY CHECK

Before approving a thumbnail, ask:

### 1-second test

Can I understand the general story in one second?

### Mobile test

Would this still work as a small YouTube thumbnail?

### Face test

Is Hardik recognizable?

### Emotion test

Does the expression communicate something?

### Curiosity test

Does the image make me ask a question?

### Contrast test

Can I distinguish foreground from background?

### Text test

Can I read the main text instantly?

### Simplicity test

Is there one dominant idea?

### Originality test

Does it feel like a new thumbnail rather than a copy?

### Wardrobe test

Does the clothing fit the topic and differ naturally from previous
thumbnails?

### AI artifact test

Are hands, eyes, teeth, glasses, text and objects believable?

If any major test fails, regenerate or edit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

-   Plastic skin

# 24. THREE-CONCEPT SYSTEM

Claude should normally generate three concepts.

## Concept A --- Safe / Proven

Strong conventional YouTube composition.

## Concept B --- High Curiosity

More unusual visual contradiction.

## Concept C --- High Emotion

Maximum emotional impact.

Then score:

  Factor                    Weight
  ----------------------- --------
  Curiosity                    25%
  Instant comprehension        20%
  Emotional impact             20%
  Mobile readability           15%
  Visual contrast              10%
  Originality                  10%

Select the highest scoring concept.

# 25. TITLE + THUMBNAIL RELATIONSHIP

The title and thumbnail should work together.

Do not repeat the same information.

Example:

### Title

"How This ₹10 Product Became a Massive Scam"

### Thumbnail

**"THEY LIED?"**

Visual: Hardik + product + suspicious evidence.

The title provides context.

The thumbnail provides emotion and curiosity.

Hardik should be recognizable in every thumbnail.
